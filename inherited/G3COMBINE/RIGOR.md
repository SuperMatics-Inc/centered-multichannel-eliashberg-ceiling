# Combined linear Migdal–Eliashberg ceilings: scope and proofs

All temperatures below are energies \(\tau=k_BT\). The results concern instability of a specified positive normal branch, not the thermodynamic transition of a Hamiltonian. A zero second moment is allowed in the counterexamples; the violations also persist with strictly positive moments. Write \(w_n=2n+1\) on the half-line, \(s_n=u_n/(\pi\tau)\), and \(b_n=\int (N(\xi)/A)u_n/[\pi(u_n^2+(\xi+\chi_n)^2)]\,d\xi\), where \(A=\|N\|_\infty\). A dimensionless phonon transfer is \(K_j=2A\int \Omega\,\alpha^2 F(d\Omega)/(\Omega^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2)\). Thus \(AM=2A\int\Omega\,\alpha^2 F(d\Omega)\); an Einstein transfer \(\lambda\Omega^2/(\Omega^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2)\) contributes \(\lambda\Omega^2\) to \(AM\). No extra DOS factor multiplies a moment defined using dimensionless \(K\).

## Q1 — PROVED for a PSD pairing pseudopotential and the covered normal equation

On the full Matsubara line and in the energy measure \(d\eta=N(\xi)d\xi\), put \(q_n(\xi)=\tau/[u_n^2+(\xi+\chi_n)^2]\). Normal time-reversal symmetry makes \(q_{-n-1}=q_n\). Let the static Coulomb kernel \(\mu^*\) be a bounded positive-semidefinite form on \(L^2(d\eta)\), and let \(h_n(\xi)\) be bounded real cutoff factors, the same in the left and right factors of the vertex. This includes no cutoff. On the conjugated gap Hilbert space \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z;L^2(d\eta))\), the Coulomb term is **subtracted**, and is \(U=L^*\mu^*L\), where \(Lf=\sum_n h_n\sqrt{q_n}f_n\). The sum defines a bounded map by Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\sum_n q_n(\xi)\le\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}|2n+1|^{-2}/(\pi^2\tau)\). Explicitly, \(\langle f,Uf\rangle=\langle Lf,\mu^*Lf\rangle\ge0\), irrespective of energy dependence or signs of entries. More general PSD Coulomb kernels on frequency × energy work by the same congruence, provided the normal branch is still covered and the form is defined. For unbounded nonnegative forms use the inequality on the common form domain. A cutoff only on an incoming index does not in general yield this self-adjoint order.

The conjugated phonon operator has blocks \((K_{|n-m|}/A)|\sqrt{q_n}\rangle\langle\sqrt{q_m}|\). Its nonzero action is exactly on the isometric image of the scalar matrix with entries \(K_{|n-m|}\sqrt{b_n b_m/(s_n s_m)}\), since \(\int q_n\,d\eta=A b_n/s_n\); it vanishes on the orthogonal fibers. The reflection-even sector controls the top: take absolute values, then combine the two reflected amplitudes at each half-line index by their root mean square. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds both the direct and reflected bilinear terms by their even replacements, without changing the norm. Hence the half-line phonon comparison from [case (e)](../G3E-GENERAL/RIGOR.md#closure-attempt) bounds the full-line spectral supremum. By the Rayleigh principle, \(\sup\sigma(B_{\rm ph}-U)\le\sup\sigma(B_{\rm ph})\). Equivalently, at the quadratic-form level the stationary normal-to-paired Hessian gains the nonnegative term \(\|\mu^{*1/2}Lf\|^2\) before restricting to the soft phonon mode. The case-(e) stability below the nominal ceiling is branch-uniform at fixed spectrum, so the conclusion is strict for the onset, not merely pointwise at \(C_\infty\sqrt{AM}\).

**Actual normal branch.** In the Eliashberg pseudopotential convention the Coulomb term is in the anomalous equation only and vanishes at the normal solution. Gauge invariance forces all neutral normal variables (including a filling constraint) to have zero derivative with respect to the charged pair field at the normal state, when differentiable. If one separately includes a frequency-independent scalar normal Hartree/Fock shift, it is real and has no odd-imaginary component. Subtracting the zeroth real normal equation cancels it; changing the chemical potential or branch at fixed density does not remove any identities required by (e). The comparison is against the phonon Hessian at the **same** actual normal state, not against the measured or computed onset on a different branch. A general energy-dependent normal Hartree/Fock shift \(\chi^C(\xi)\) is not a scalar: for an energy-independent phonon vertex one can push the DOS forward by \(\xi\mapsto\xi+\chi^C(\xi)\), then apply (e) if that DOS has a bounded density with **its own** height. There need not be any preservation of the old DOS height (e.g. compression of the dispersion). An arbitrary frequency-dependent normal self-energy is not fixed by the pairing PSD condition; the result with the original DOS bound is not asserted for such a closure. The constant for the class is sharp even when \(\mu^*\ne0\) is required: at each strict unstable shelf fixture in case (e), scale any nonzero bounded PSD pseudopotential small enough compared with that fixture’s positive spectral margin. This is not sharpness for every fixed Coulomb operator.

## Q2 — reciprocal moments, a sharp common-branch theorem, and the remaining general case

**Model.** Write \(x,y\) for surface channels, \(d\nu\) for the reference angular measure, and \(A(x)\) for a bound on the channel DOS density in energy. Omit zero-height channels. For reciprocal bare spectral kernels independent of both energies within their channel, put \(d\eta(x)=A(x)d\nu(x)\), and integrate the symmetric bare transfer \(K_j(x,y)=\int d\mathfrak m_{xy}(\Omega)/(\Omega^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2)\) against \(d\eta(y)\). Here \(d\mathfrak m_{xy}=2\Omega\alpha^2 F_{xy}(d\Omega)\) in this bare-vertex convention. On \(L^2(d\eta)\), \(\mathcal M\) is the operator with kernel \(\int d\mathfrak m_{xy}\); define \(\rho=\sup\sigma(\mathcal M)=\|\mathcal M\|\), by self-adjointness and the absolute-value comparison for nonnegative kernels. In discrete channels this is the spectral radius of the moment matrix **including target-channel DOS weights**; use the reciprocal similar matrix, not the entrywise maximum. The maximum row integral is a possibly larger Schur bound, \(\rho\le R=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_x (\mathcal M1)(x)\) when finite. When all heights equal the same value \(A\), these are precisely \(A\) times the moment norm and row bound in the unweighted angular measure. Assume bounded zero-lag and moment row integrals so the normal equations and forms exist; assume reflection-symmetric normal self-energies on the Matsubara line. These suffice for the statements below (including finite channel models).

**PROVED: general nonsharp norm ceiling.** The energy conjugation from Q1 works fiberwise. On the half-line write \(D_{nm}=K_{|n-m|}\), \(H_{nm}=K_{n+m+1}\), \(P=D-H\ge0\) pointwise, and \(s_n(x)=w_n+\sum_m\int P_{nm}(x,y)b_m(y)d\eta(y)\). Substitute the normal equation in the pairing form using conjugated amplitudes \(f_n(x)=\sqrt{b_n(x)s_n(x)}\,v_n(x)\), to get
\[
 Q=\sum_n\int w_n b_n|v_n|^2d\eta
 +\frac12\sum_{n,m}\iint P_{nm} b_n(x)b_m(y)|v_n(x)-v_m(y)|^2d\eta(x)d\eta(y)
 -2\sum_{n,m}\iint H_{nm}b_n(x)b_m(y)\Re(\overline{v_n(x)}v_m(y))d\eta(x)d\eta(y).
\]
The expansion is valid first on finite-support bounded test functions, then by form continuity; zero-weight fibers contribute no pairing. The factor of two in the last term is from \(D+H=P+2H\). Drop the nonnegative second term and bound \(H_{nm}(x,y)\le \mathcal M(x,y)/[(2\pi\tau)^2(n+m+1)^2]\). Using \(b\le1\) and the angular norm, the last term in absolute value is at most \(2\rho\|H_s\|/(2\pi\tau)^2\) times the first, where \((H_s)_{nm}=1/[\sqrt{w_n w_m}(n+m+1)^2]\). The Schur test with weights \(1/\sqrt{w_n}\) gives \(\|H_s\|\le\sum_{m\ge0}1/[(2m+1)(m+1)^2]=4\log2-\pi^2/6\), by partial fractions. Thus \(\tau_{\rm lin}\le\sqrt{(4\log2-\pi^2/6)/(2\pi^2)}\sqrt\rho\). A positive relative form gap excludes even onset (and by reflection the competing phonon odd or complex modes). This bound uses no comparison of the different angular normal branches or real shifts. It is not the claimed sharp bound.

Indeed \(s_n(x)\le w_n+D_*\), where \(D_*\) bounds the row integral of \(K_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}K_j\). Thus the first term is at least \(\|f\|^2/(1+D_*)\), giving a gap in the original pairing operator whenever the Hankel coefficient is strictly below one.

**PROVED: exactly sharp on common branches with balanced transfers.** Assume \(0<\eta(X)=\int_X A(x)d\nu(x)<\infty\). Suppose every transfer \(K_j\) has an angle-independent row integral \(\bar K_j\), and the normalized channel DOS profiles are common, permitting the common self-consistent branch (translations of the bare channels are allowed if their effective profiles on that branch agree). On that branch \(s_n,b_n\) are independent of angle; the common relative real and imaginary equations are precisely (e) with spectrum given by the row-integrated transfer. For each \(j\), the angular norm equals \(\bar K_j\) by the Schur test and the constant direction in \(L^2(d\eta)\). Testing the Rayleigh quotient block by block against the angular norms of the frequency amplitudes gives the scalar top as an upper bound. Taking angular constants gives the reverse inequality. Here \(\rho=R\) equals the common moment and the coefficient \(C_\infty\) is sharp by the one-channel asymmetric sequence, or by placing it on any fixed regular angular kernel with the same measure and positive constant row sum. We make no assumption that all other normal solutions in this balanced model must be common.

Uniform flat DOS fixes \(b=1\) on every branch. For that model, the Nov. 16 GSI comparison [Theorems 7–9](https://www.generalised.si/research/superconducting-tc-ceiling) states the sharp \(C_\infty\sqrt\rho\) norm bound (strict for finitely many band indices, nonstrict in the continuum). Theorem 8 brackets the fixed-matrix supremum between \(C_\infty\sqrt{R^*}\) and \(C_\infty\sqrt{R^*/(1-3.6\times10^{-5})}\), where \(R^*\) is the largest moment Rayleigh quotient of a nonempty subset indicator; its pure-interband example has a nonzero correction, so the lower endpoint is not an exact general formula. Theorem 9 gives an analogous continuum bracket. Theorem 10 also gives the flat pairing-only PSD comparison. These results do not cover arbitrary channel-dependent energy DOS. If only a *double average* is retained, a patch of angular measure \(p\) with interaction \(p^{-1}K\) inside the patch and zero otherwise keeps \(R\), \(\rho\), and the scalar branch/instability in the patch, but reduces the double average by \(p\). This refutes a ceiling using the double average, even with flat DOS.

The scalar theorem also applies blockwise to uncoupled channels with different DOS profiles: the overall top is their supremum, and the overall moment norm is the supremum of the block moments. For a finite collection of scalar channels this gives the strict ceiling without any common-branch condition.

**OPEN: sharp bound for the general energy-independent surface extension.** Flat-DOS normal cancellation uses \(b_n(x)=1\), while (e) uses one complex Cauchy transform and the scalar same-channel local term \(K_0G_n\). In the angular equations the zero-lag normal term instead applies the operator \(K_0\) to \(G_n(\cdot)\); \(b_n(x)\) and the real shifts vary by angle and frequency. Using \(b\le1\) separately on positive and negative terms in the flat comparison is not valid on the negative mass terms. In fact even the one-channel relaxed width equation (but not its real equation) admits the mutant \(b_0=1\), \(b_m=0\) for \(m>0\): its \(n=0\) test of \(s-b(D+H)\) is \(1-2K_1\), negative for \(K_1>1/2\) at the nominal moment scale (soft Einstein transfers). Thus an argument that discards the normal DOS constraint cannot prove the desired coefficient. The common-branch approach and the full cancellation/norm approach establish the two results above but do not supply the missing comparison for coupled, energy-dependent normal branches. No counterexample in this energy-independent-vertex model is claimed.

**COUNTEREXAMPLE: a Fermi-surface-only moment for a full-band, energy-dependent vertex.** Take flat DOS of height 1 on \([-1000,1000]\). Set the electron–phonon vertex to zero whenever either energy lies in \(|\xi|\le1/10\), and otherwise take the Einstein kernel \(K_j=8/(1+j^2)\) at \(\tau=1\) (\(\Omega=2\pi\)). This is symmetric and nonnegative, is identically zero on the actual Fermi surface, and on the active sector reduces to the even scalar DOS \(1_{1/10<|\xi|<1000}\). The real shift vanishes for the centered symmetric normal branch, which exists by the fixed-point construction in Q3 (no need for uniqueness of this notched-band variant). The complementary Fermi sector is decoupled and free. For every centered positive branch at \(\tau=1\), \(1\le s_0\le9\) and
\[
 b_0\ge1-2s_0/1000-2(1/10)/(\pi^2 s_0)>3/4.
\]
The subtractions are respectively the Poisson tail outside the band and the center notch. The one-mode **unnormalized form margin** is \(m=(K_0+K_1)b_0-s_0=2K_1 b_0-1-\sum_{m'\ge1}(K_{m'}-K_{m'+1})b_{m'}\ge K_1(2b_0-1)-1\). Using \(\pi^2>9\) and \(1\le s_0\le9\) in the Poisson bound gives \(m\ge3013/1125\). The pairing Rayleigh quotient is \(1+m/s_0\ge1+(3013/1125)/9=13138/10125>1\). No frequency cutoff was made. A strictly positive but arbitrarily small surface moment can be added in the decoupled central sector without changing this witness or the total DOS height. Therefore a theorem for arbitrary **full-band interactions** cannot use their value restricted to the Fermi surface, even when the DOS itself is flat in a finite band. This does not refute the reduced angular model immediately above, in which the vertex has no independent off-shell dependence.

The same witness rules out using \(N(0)\), rather than the DOS height, even when the vertex **is** constant in energy: instead keep the Einstein vertex active throughout the band and fill the central notch up to height \(0<\varepsilon\le1\). Now \(N(0)=\varepsilon\), \(A=1\), and \(s_0\le9\); the Poisson lower bound above is unchanged, since the added DOS is nonnegative. The moment formed using \(N(0)\) tends to zero with \(\varepsilon\), while the unit-temperature instability persists. This is a counterexample to that normalization, not to the proposed height-weighted sharp bound.

## Q3 — COUNTEREXAMPLE with causal dynamical screening and no frequency cutoff

Use a centered finite box \(N=1_{|\xi|<W}\), \(W=10^{10}\) in the same energy units as the witness \(\tau=1\). Use a phonon Einstein coupling \(\lambda_p=1/8\), \(0<\Omega_p\le1\), so its DOS-weighted moment is \(AM_p=\Omega_p^2/8\). In the same DOS normalization take the pointwise repulsive transfer
\[
 W_C(i\nu)=U-\frac{4E^2}{E^2+\nu^2},\qquad U=5,\quad E=2\pi.
\]
It is everywhere \(\ge1\); the upper-half-plane continuation \(W_C^R(z)=5-4E^2/(E^2-z^2)\) is retarded and has nonnegative positive-frequency loss \(-\Im W_C^R\) (a delta peak). This is the screened-repulsion sign, not a positive mixture of Lorentzians added to a bare repulsion. In a scalar exchange Eliashberg closure the instantaneous piece contributes at most a real static shift (absorbed in centering), while the screening term is a positive contribution to the odd normal mass, exactly like the phonon. This follows directly from inserting the attractive part of the *pairing* transfer \(-W_C\) in the odd normal convolution: a constant cancels under odd reflection. The even pairing transfer includes both positive Lorentzians with \(K_j=4E^2/[E^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2]+K^p_j\), **minus** the static 5 on the full line, or minus 10 on the folded line. Energy reflection sets the relative real shift to zero. This gives normal widths from the very same screened interaction that produces the pair vertex.

**Normal branch and high-temperature closure.** At each \(\tau>0\), \(b_n(s)=(2/\pi)\arctan(W/(\pi\tau s_n))\), and \(s=w+P b(s)\). The finite coordinate map (truncate the normal convolution temporarily) sends the compact box \(w_n\le s_n\le w_n+K_0+2\sum_{j\ge1}K_j\) into itself, by the full-line row bound. Brouwer, diagonal extraction and dominated convergence in each summable row yield an infinite centered branch. This existence argument also works for the nonmonotone notched-box \(b\) of Q2. In the Q3 box, \(b(s)\) strictly decreases with width. The odd compression \(P\) is PSD: \(E^2/(E^2+(2\pi\tau j)^2)\) is the Fourier transform evaluated at \(2\pi\tau j\) of \((E/2)e^{-E|t|}dt\); the Toeplitz form is nonnegative and the compression is its odd sector. For two solutions, \(\delta b\in\ell^2\) from \(b_n\le 2W/(\pi^2\tau w_n)\), \(\delta s=P\delta b\), and \(\langle\delta b,\delta s\rangle\le0\) strictly if the solutions differ, whereas the PSD form is \(\ge0\). Hence the centered branch is unique in widths. Coordinatewise continuity in \(\tau>0\) follows by diagonal compactness and uniqueness, with local summable transfer domination. Then \(a_n=b_n/s_n\le2W/(\pi^2\tau w_n^2)\) gives dominated Hilbert–Schmidt continuity of the symmetrized pairing matrix, including its Coulomb part. It also gives high-temperature stability: since every folded transfer is bounded uniformly in \(\tau\), the matrix norm is \(O(W/\tau)\). Thus a strict instability at \(\tau=1\) has an actual positive-temperature crossing above the witness on this branch.

**Exact instability witness.** At \(\tau=1\), \(K_0\le33/8\), \(K_0+K_1\ge6\), and \(K_j\le(33/8)/j^2\) for \(j\ge1\), since \(\Omega_p\le1<2\pi\). Thus \(s_n<w_n+21\) by \(\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-2}<2\), and \(s_0\le1+33/8\) by the telescoped first normal row. Set the real gap trial in weighted coordinates \(z_0=1\); take \(L=16\) blocks \(N_i\le n<2N_i\), \(N_i=32\cdot2^i\) for \(0\le i<L\), and set \(z_n=-1/(L N_i)\) there, zero elsewhere. Its exact sum is zero, so the instantaneous contribution vanishes identically despite \(U=5\). The gap instability criterion is
\[
 z^*(D+H)z-2U|\textstyle\sum z_n|^2-\sum_n (s_n/b_n)|z_n|^2>0;
\]
this is the Rayleigh form after the invertible coordinate change on the finite support. Only the trial, not the normal or pairing operator, is cut off. On its support \(b_n\ge1-2s_n/W>99/100\). On the tail \(s_n<(4/3)w_n\), since \(w_n\ge65\). Each dyadic block has \(\sum w_n=3N_i^2\); the total tail normal cost is bounded by \(4/[L(99/100)]\). All tail-tail transfer terms are nonnegative. The cross term from the zeroth index costs at most \(4(33/8)/32^2\), because its total opposing amplitude is one and each contributing transfer is bounded at lag at least 32. Therefore the witness margin is at least
\[
 6-\frac{(41/8)}{99/100}-\frac4{16(99/100)}-\frac{4(33/8)}{32^2}=\frac{112445}{202752}>\frac12.
\]
This is an unnormalized form margin. The squared norm of the trial in the symmetrized pairing coordinates is \(\sum_n(s_n/b_n)|z_n|^2\le(41/8+4/16)/(99/100)=1075/198\). Dividing the margin by this norm and adding one gives the pairing Rayleigh quotient lower bound \(242649/220160>1\).

In particular \(\tau_{\rm lin}>1>C_\infty\sqrt{AM_p}\) for the entire phonon family, and the ratio is unbounded as \(\Omega_p\downarrow0\). This is a generated ME counterexample, not a prediction for a specific material. The instability is even in frequency (its sign changes across the *positive* frequencies). The same trial proves non-PSD of the screened kernel on the static-null space: the convolution of the attractive screening Lorentzian by itself has a strictly positive quadratic form for every nonzero finite trial, by the Fourier representation (a nonzero trigonometric polynomial cannot vanish almost everywhere). The negative of that form is the only surviving screened-repulsion term there. If the retarded screening attraction is counted in the *total* positive moment, this example does **not** violate the corresponding ceiling: the instantaneous pairing term is then a PSD subtraction and Q1 applies to the combined attractive spectrum. Likewise a pairing-only positive Stieltjes repulsion \(U+\int E^2/(E^2+\nu^2)d\gamma(E)\) with \(U,d\gamma\ge0\) is PSD by the same Fourier representation and does not give this mechanism on a covered normal branch.

**Positioning.** [GSI's dynamical-screening example](https://www.generalised.si/research/superconducting-tc-ceiling) reaches about \(1.83\,C_\infty\sqrt M\) with a sharp cutoff and a pairing-only kernel, but drops numerically to \(0.955\,C_\infty\sqrt M\) when that kernel also enters mass renormalization. Our witness includes the screening in the self-consistent mass and has no cutoff, but uses deliberately strong dimensionless model parameters: \(U=5\), screening decrement \(4\), and \(W_C(0)=1\), versus, for example, a calculated Coulomb parameter \(\mu=0.62\) in [Bozier–Pickard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12563). The same source quotes typical empirical \(\mu^*=0.10\)–\(0.15\); these are retarded pseudopotentials, not the bare \(U\) or the screening decrement. The witness does not establish violations for realistic materials or realistic couplings.

## Trust, attacks, and replay boundaries

- Q1 imports the branch-uniform scalar theorem (e); the energy-space congruence and static-shift argument are analytic. Frequency cutoffs must be applied on both factors and do not enter the example for Q3. Entrywise positivity does not prove PSD.
- Q2's flat comparator is prior art, not independently certified here. The new common-branch proof does not compare different self-consistent branches. The negative mass term invalidates a \(b\le1\) flat reduction; the normal-width-only mutant is an obstruction to that proof, not to (e). The full-band example relies on off-shell freedom explicitly excluded by the reduced model.
- Q2 off-shell and Q3 instability inequalities use exact arithmetic with integer/rational bounds in the two replays. They do not solve finite-cutoff normal equations or approximate infinite tails. The analytic normal-branch existence, high-temperature behavior, causal continuation, Poisson estimates, and spectral comparisons are proved above, not replayed. `Finite.lean` only checks finite algebra/signs with these analytic bounds external.
