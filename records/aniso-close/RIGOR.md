## 1. Actual branch, sharp route screens, and honest negatives

Write w_n = 2n+1 and t = (E/(2 pi tau))^2. The dimensionless unit-moment transfers are k_j = 1/(t+j^2), p_nm = k_|n-m| - k_(n+m+1), and S_nm = k_|n-m| + k_(n+m+1). The physical transfer multiplies dm_xy(E)/(2 pi tau)^2. With dimensionless electronic energy a = xi/(pi tau),

\[
 b_x(s)=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\nu_x(\pi\tau a)\frac{s}{s^2+a^2}\,da,
 \qquad g_x(s)=b_x(s)/s.
\]

The actual normal branch is s_nx = w_n + (2 pi tau)^(-2) sum_y eta_y integral sum_m p_nm b_my dm_xy. Pairing contains sqrt(b_nx b_my/(s_nx s_my)); treating b and s independently loses essential feedback.

Three proposed sharp routes were screened, not promoted to theorems:

1. **Channel Jensen / concavity in g or sqrt(g): fails as a general pointwise step.** For one electronic energy a, f(s) = 1/(a^2+s^2) has f''(s) = 2(3s^2-a^2)/(a^2+s^2)^3, which is positive for s > |a|/sqrt(3). The square root has second derivative (2s^2-a^2)/(a^2+s^2)^(5/2), positive for s > |a|/sqrt(2). Integrating over DOS supported in a bounded interval preserves this positive sign at sufficiently large s. Even the full flat profile gives g=1/s and sqrt(g)=s^(-1/2), both convex. These are exact counter-mechanisms to universal concavity, not counterexamples to the coupled ceiling. A channel average generally changes both DOS and widths, so a one-variable Jensen step cannot dispose of the feedback terms.
2. **Monotone filling to flat DOS: unavailable.** `../SHARP/RIGOR.md` §7 records negative DOS-level derivatives of both gain and onset in centered two-channel models. This is an inherited numerical obstruction to a pointwise monotonicity argument, not an exact proof that globally flat DOS is nonextremal. This continuation screens DOS-bin changes on models generated by search_adv.py; only the concrete finite-resolution observations in CHECKS.txt are used.
3. **Channel-uniform trial / min–max without branch locking: insufficient.** A restricted trial Rayleigh quotient is a lower bound on the spectral top, not an upper bound. A proof by an upper comparison must show a valid operator domination or control the omitted channel fluctuations. Flat DOS, the common soft upper asymptotic, and common-DOS all-row balance already have inherited sharp results (`../ANISO-SHARP/RIGOR.md` §1; `../SHARPFLAT4/RIGOR.md`). All-row operational balance collapses to transfer balance and is not a larger unequal-DOS subclass. A narrow scalar/flat reduction does not establish a new theorem for two arbitrary coupled channels.

The short continuation screen uses the existing finite-resolution normal solver, not a new physical model. It does not certify convergence of its extrapolated normal tail. No full 2,400-model rerun is substituted for an analytic sharp proof.
